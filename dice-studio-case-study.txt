 CASE STUDY From Creative Showcase to Client-Generating Machine How We Helped DICE Studio Grow Bookings by 180% in 90 Days Executive Summary DICE Studio is a multi-disciplinary creative collective working across interior design, themed spatial experiences, documentary film, mural art, and digital content. Their work was consistently excellent — but their website wasn&apos;t doing anything with the attention it earned. Visitors browsed a strong portfolio, felt impressed, and left. There was no clear way to book a consultation, no defined path from “interested” to “client,” and no internal system to catch and respond to the leads that did trickle in. We rebuilt the front end of DICE Studio&apos;s client acquisition process from the ground up: a structured booking funnel, automated scheduling, a new e-commerce revenue stream, and hands-on training so the internal team could actually run the system we built. Within 90 days, monthly consultation bookings were up 180%, site conversion rate climbed from 0.8% to 3.4%, and average lead response time dropped from over 24 hours to under 15 minutes. E-commerce alone added a 25% lift to top-line revenue in the first six months. This is the story of how that happened — and why it&apos;s held up since. About the Client DICE Studio doesn&apos;t fit neatly into one category, which is part of what makes their work distinctive. On any given month, their team might be designing the interior of a boutique hotel, building out an immersive themed installation for a corporate activation, shooting a short documentary, painting a mural for a commercial client, or producing branded digital content — often for the same client, across several of those disciplines at once. That range is a genuine strength. Clients come to DICE Studio because they can get a cohesive creative vision executed across physical space, film, and digital media without stitching together three or four separate vendors. But it also created a specific business problem: a portfolio that impressive doesn&apos;t sell itself just because it&apos;s impressive. It needs a system behind it. The Challenge When we started working with DICE Studio, the issue wasn&apos;t demand for their work — it was what happened to that demand once it showed up. No clear way to book anything The website had contact information and a portfolio, but no defined consultation or booking flow. Prospective clients who wanted to move forward had to send a cold email or make a phone call and hope for a timely reply. For a studio whose services span five disciplines, that ambiguity was compounded — visitors often weren&apos;t sure which service to even ask about, let alone how. No structured calls-to-action or user paths The site was built to showcase, not to convert. There was no consistent CTA guiding visitors toward a next step, and no differentiated path for someone interested in, say, a themed installation versus someone interested in a mural commission. Interested prospects browsed, admired the work, and left without a clear reason to act before they lost momentum. Communication bottlenecks internally Even when leads did come in, there was no standardized process for triaging them, assigning ownership, or following up on a consistent timeline. Inquiries sat in a shared inbox. Response times varied by whoever happened to check email that day, which meant some prospects waited over 24 hours for a reply — long enough for a lot of them to have already booked somewhere else. Put together, DICE Studio had a strong top of funnel and almost nothing built to carry that interest through to a signed project. The Solution &amp; Transformation We approached this as an end-to-end system build, not a website refresh. The goal was to give DICE Studio&apos;s creative work a commercial engine that matched its quality. 1. A High-Converting Lead &amp; Booking Funnel We designed and built a structured funnel with a single, unambiguous primary action — “Book Free Consultancy” — placed consistently across the site rather than competing with a scattershot of secondary links. Behind that CTA sat a short, guided path: visitors selected the discipline they were interested in (interior design, themed spaces, documentary, mural, digital content), answered a few qualifying questions about their project, and landed directly on a booking calendar. This did two things at once. It gave every visitor a clear next step regardless of which part of DICE Studio&apos;s work brought them to the site, and it gave the DICE Studio team pre-qualified context on every booked call before they ever picked up the phone. 2. Automated Scheduling &amp; Lead Management We integrated an automated scheduling system directly into the funnel, so a consultation could be booked in a couple of clicks without a single back-and-forth email. Every inquiry — booked or not — now flows into a centralized lead management system rather than a shared inbox, tagged by service line and automatically routed to the right team member. This is the piece that made the response-time improvement possible. Instead of leads waiting for someone to notice them, the system notifies the right person the moment an inquiry comes in. 3. An E-Commerce Layer for Physical Goods DICE Studio&apos;s work naturally produces artifacts with standalone value: custom props from installations, original artwork, mural prints, branded merchandise. We built out an e-commerce section to sell these directly, turning creative byproducts into an independent revenue stream. This wasn&apos;t just about incremental sales. It gave DICE Studio a lower-commitment entry point for people who weren&apos;t ready to book a full project but wanted to own a piece of the work — and a meaningful share of those buyers went on to become project leads once they had a tangible reason to engage further. 4. Team Training &amp; Communication Enablement None of this holds up if the team can&apos;t run it. We spent time on-site and remotely training DICE Studio&apos;s staff on the new booking system, the lead management dashboard, and a standardized communication process — including response-time expectations and templated but personalized reply frameworks for common inquiry types. The point wasn&apos;t to hand over a tool and leave. It was to make sure the system kept working after we stepped back, which is the difference between a project and a partnership. Conclusion &amp; Client Takeaway DICE Studio&apos;s creative output was never the problem. What they lacked was the connective tissue between “impressive portfolio” and “booked, paying client” — a gap that&apos;s common among creative studios whose work is visual and experiential by nature but whose growth depends on decidedly unglamorous fundamentals: clear CTAs, fast follow-up, and systems that don&apos;t rely on someone remembering to check an inbox. What we built together was designed to last past our direct involvement. The booking funnel, the lead management system, and the e-commerce channel are all things DICE Studio&apos;s team now owns and runs themselves, with a process for training new hires into it as the studio grows. That&apos;s the model we work from broadly: we&apos;re not in the business of shipping a nicer-looking website and moving on. We build the operational systems that turn creative and professional work into predictable revenue — and we make sure the team behind that work can run the system without us in the room.
